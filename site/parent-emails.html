Before you start

Decide: purpose of the email (classroom update, missing work, event reminder, praise, concern); what parents need to know (dates, times, actions); and what parents need to do.

Never include student names, grades, or personal information in the AI prompt. Use placeholders like "[Student's first name]" or "your child" and fill them in yourself afterward. See Privacy and safety.

Step by step

  1. Open a new chat in your free AI tool.
  2. Describe the email. Say who it is to, the purpose, and the key details.
  3. Set the tone. Ask for "friendly, plain English, short" or "warm and encouraging."
  4. Review the draft. Read it out loud. Check dates and times. Make sure nothing sounds like medical or legal advice.
  5. Fill in placeholders yourself. Replace "[Student's first name]" and similar placeholders in your email program — not in the AI tool.
  6. Send from your own email. Copy the final text into your school email and send.

Example prompt (copy and adapt)

I am a fifth-grade teacher. Draft a short, friendly email to parents reminding them about the class field trip next Friday. Include: departure time 9:00 a.m., packed lunch needed, permission slips due Wednesday, and a request for two chaperones. Use plain English. Do not include any student names.

Tone tips

  • Lead with the positive. Start with something good before asking for help or raising a concern.
  • One request per email. Parents act more often when an email asks for one thing.
  • Give a clear deadline. "Please return the slip by Wednesday" works better than "soon."
  • No medical or legal advice. AI is not qualified for health or legal matters. Have an administrator review those emails.
