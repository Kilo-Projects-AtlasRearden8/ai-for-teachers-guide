Before you start

Have these details ready: grade level, subject, topic or standard, class length, materials you have, and what students should know by the end.

Do not include student names, grades, or personal information. Refer to "the class" or "students" only. See Privacy and safety.

Step by step

  1. Open a new chat in any free AI tool (see Free AI models).
  2. Give context. Say you are a teacher and what grade/subject you teach.
  3. Ask for the lesson plan. Request objectives, materials, warm-up, main activity, wrap-up, and assessment.
  4. Review the draft. Check facts, timing, and reading level. Simplify anything too advanced.
  5. Edit in your own words. Add your voice and classroom routines.
  6. Save or print. Copy into a document, or fill out the printable lesson plan template.

Example prompt (copy and adapt)

I am a sixth-grade science teacher. Write a 45-minute lesson plan on photosynthesis for middle school. Include learning objectives, a materials list, a 5-minute warm-up, a 20-minute main activity, a 10-minute group discussion, and a 5-minute exit-ticket question. Align to general middle-school life science standards. Keep instructions simple and student-friendly.

What to watch for

  • AI makes mistakes. Check science facts, dates, and math before handing anything out.
  • Timing may be off. A "10-minute activity" can take 20 minutes in a real classroom. Plan buffer time.
  • Differentiation. AI drafts assume one level. Add notes for students who need more support or more challenge.
