# 6. Parent Emails

AI can draft warm, clear emails to parents. You add the details, check the tone, and send from your own account.

## Before you start

Decide:

- Purpose of the email (classroom update, missing work, event reminder, praise, concern)
- What parents need to know (dates, times, actions needed)
- What parents need to do (sign a form, send supplies, attend a meeting)

**Never include student names, grades, or personal information in the AI prompt.** Use placeholders like "[Student's first name]" or "your child" and fill them in yourself after the draft.

## Step-by-step

**Step 1. Open a new chat** in your free AI tool.

**Step 2. Describe the email.** Say who it is to (a parent or guardian), the purpose, and the key details.

**Step 3. Set the tone.** Ask for "friendly, plain English, short" or "warm and encouraging." Avoid jargon.

**Step 4. Review the draft.** Read it out loud. Make sure dates and times are right. Make sure nothing sounds like a diagnosis, accusation, or medical advice.

**Step 5. Fill in placeholders yourself.** Replace "[Student's first name]" and similar placeholders with real details in your email program — not in the AI tool.

**Step 6. Send from your own email.** Copy the final text into your school email and send.

## Example prompt (no student data)

> I am a fifth-grade teacher. Draft a short, friendly email to parents reminding them about the class field trip next Friday. Include: departure time 9:00 a.m., packed lunch needed, permission slips due Wednesday, and a request for two chaperones. Use plain English. Do not include any student names.

## Tone tips

- **Lead with the positive.** Start with something good before asking for help or raising a concern.
- **One request per email.** Parents are more likely to act when the email asks for one thing.
- **Give a clear deadline.** "Please return the slip by Wednesday" works better than "soon."
- **No medical or legal advice.** AI is not qualified to advise on student health or legal matters. If an email touches on those areas, have an administrator review it.
