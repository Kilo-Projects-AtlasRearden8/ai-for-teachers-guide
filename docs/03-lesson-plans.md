# 3. Lesson Plans

AI can write a first draft of a lesson plan in seconds. You still review, fix, and personalize it. This guide shows you how, step by step.

## Before you start

Have these details ready:

- Grade level and subject
- Topic or standard you are teaching
- Class length (minutes)
- Any materials you already have or want to use
- What students should know by the end (objective)

**Do not include student names, grades, or any personal information.** Refer to "the class" or "students" only.

## Step-by-step

**Step 1. Open a new chat.** Use any free AI tool (see `02-free-ai-models.md`).

**Step 2. Give the AI context.** Tell it you are a teacher and what grade/subject you teach.

**Step 3. Ask for the lesson plan.** Be specific about length and parts you want: objectives, materials, warm-up, main activity, wrap-up, and assessment.

**Step 4. Review the draft.** Read every line. Check facts. Make sure the timing adds up. Simplify language that is too advanced.

**Step 5. Edit in your own words.** Add your voice, your classroom routines, and anything your students need.

**Step 6. Save or print.** Copy into a document, or fill out the printable template in `docs/templates/lesson-plan.md`.

## Example prompt (no student data)

> I am a sixth-grade science teacher. Write a 45-minute lesson plan on photosynthesis for middle school. Include learning objectives, a materials list, a 5-minute warm-up, a 20-minute main activity, a 10-minute group discussion, and a 5-minute exit-ticket question. Align to general middle-school life science standards. Keep instructions simple and student-friendly.

## What to watch for

- **AI makes mistakes.** Check science facts, dates, and math before handing anything out.
- **Timing may be off.** A "10-minute activity" can take 20 minutes in a real classroom. Plan buffer time.
- **Differentiation.** AI drafts often assume one level. Add notes for students who need more support or more challenge.
