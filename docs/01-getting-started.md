# 1. Getting Started

Welcome. This guide walks you through using AI for teaching, from your first prompt to your first printable handout. No technical background needed. Everything here works with free AI models.

## What AI is, in plain English

AI chat tools are programs you type to, and they type back. They are good at patterns: drafting, rewording, summarizing, and brainstorming. They are not perfect. They can get facts wrong, so you should always check anything you plan to hand to students or send to parents.

Think of AI as a very fast teaching assistant who writes first drafts. You are still the teacher. You decide what is accurate and appropriate.

## Ways teachers use AI

- Writing lesson plans
- Making worksheets and handouts
- Writing quiz questions and answer keys
- Drafting emails to parents
- Rewriting reading passages at different levels
- Brainstorming activities for students who finish early

## Step 1: Pick a free AI tool

See `02-free-ai-models.md` for a list of free options. Look for:

- A free tier (no credit card required to start)
- A web interface you can open in your browser
- An option to turn off training on your conversations (see `07-privacy-safety.md`)

## Step 2: Create an account safely

Most free tools ask for an email address. That is usually enough. You do not need to give your school name, your students' names, or your payment information just to try the free tier.

Use a strong, unique password. If your school has rules about which AI tools staff may use, check with your administrator first.

## Step 3: Write your first prompt

A prompt is just the message you send the AI. Give it:

1. **Who you are** — "I am a fifth-grade teacher."
2. **What you want** — "Write a 30-minute lesson plan about the water cycle."
3. **Details** — grade level, class length, any standards you follow.
4. **Format** — "Include objectives, materials, and a short activity."

**Example prompt (no student data):**

> I am a fourth-grade teacher. Write a 45-minute lesson plan on introductory fractions. Include learning objectives, a materials list, a 10-minute warm-up, a main activity, and a 5-minute wrap-up. Keep the language simple.

## Step 4: Check and edit the output

Read everything the AI gives you. Fix any mistakes, simplify anything too hard for your students, and add your own voice. Never hand out AI output without reading it first.

## Step 5: Save or print

Copy the result into a document, or use the printable templates in `docs/templates/` and `site/templates/`. Print directly from your browser (see `08-printable-templates.md`).

## The golden rule (read this first)

**Never paste student names, grades, ID numbers, or any personal student information into an AI tool.** See `07-privacy-safety.md` for the full privacy rules.
